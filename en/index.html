Station brief

Start from a concrete scene, then set out the questions, methods and boundaries of this station.

Gold in a vault, a gold ETF on an exchange, and a gold futures contract with margin and an expiry date are all called “gold”. Their claims, cash flows and exits are completely different. This station starts by making those differences visible.

Read a product on four layers: what the underlying asset is, what claim the investor receives, how it trades and settles, and where cost, leverage and exit conditions sit. If any layer is blurry, a familiar name makes the mistake easier.

Equities, bonds, funds, commodities, currencies and derivatives each have their own price drivers. Share prices follow residual profit; bond prices follow rates and credit; futures prices follow the spot asset, the curve and margin.

Derivatives split risk and add leverage. Daily mark-to-market in futures, the premium in options and counterparty exposure in swaps are part of the product, not footnotes.

A product is not a strategy. A low-volatility product inside a leveraged portfolio can become high risk; a volatile product in a small position may not be dangerous. Understand the tool before arguing about allocation.

After this station, you should be able to take a product document and name its underlying asset, investor claim, main costs, leverage and exit conditions.

Four common misreadings

These claims come up again and again in reader mail, comments and working discussions. Naming them is more useful than repeating definitions.

“Same name, same risk.”

The same underlying can be wrapped in different legal structures: physical, fund shares, futures, options, structured notes. Claims, leverage, liquidity and counterparties differ, so risk differs.

“ETFs do not trade at discounts.”

Authorised participants’ creation and redemption normally narrow premiums and discounts. When liquidity is stressed, the underlying is halted or creation is restricted, the gap can widen and persist.

“Futures are gambling.”

Futures began as a way to transfer price risk, with hedgers, speculators and market makers supplying liquidity. Leverage turns ordinary volatility into margin pressure; that is the contract structure at work.

“Gold always hedges risk.”

Gold responds to real rates, the dollar, liquidity and carrying costs. In an early crisis, investors may need cash and sell gold too. Its hedge behaviour is conditional, not constant.

Gold is flat, so why do two gold products differ by 2%?

Three or four articles from this station, applied to one decision. Every number is a teaching assumption for practising the order of judgement, not an estimate of a real market.

At one moment gold is quoted at 500 yuan a gram. A shop sells bars at 510, including fabrication and distribution; a gold ETF trades at 500.5 in the secondary market; a futures contract trades at 502, including carry and margin. None of the three quotes is “the gold price” itself.

The ETF has creation and redemption, so premiums usually get arbitraged away, but spreads widen when liquidity is thin. Futures require margin, and long holding periods can be worn down by roll costs.

Physical gold carries storage, assay and buy-back discounts. In a hurry, a dealer may pay 3–5% below the posted price; that cost never appears in the daily quote.

Break “buying gold” into five items: holding form, quote source, bid-ask spread, storage or roll cost, and exit route. Set out all five before choosing the one that fits your horizon and purpose.

Key terms

These words recur throughout the station. Learn the sense used here before moving into the articles.

asset 资产

A source or store of value such as cash, shares, bonds, property or gold; distinct from the instrument, which is the legal wrapper used to hold it.

MKT-001 · Assets and Instruments
bond 债券

A debt security of the issuer; the holder receives coupons and principal under the documents. It is not an ownership claim.

MKT-004 · Bond Rights and Cash Flows
yield to maturity 到期收益率

The discount rate that makes the present value of future cash flows equal the current price; state the coupon frequency and reinvestment assumptions.

MKT-004 · Bond Rights and Cash Flows
ETF ETF

A fund share traded on an exchange; authorised participants handle creation and redemption while ordinary investors trade in the secondary market.

MKT-007 · ETF Creation, Redemption and Premiums
margin 保证金

Collateral posted to cover potential futures losses; positions are marked daily and shortfalls trigger calls or liquidation.

MKT-018 · Futures Contracts and Margin
basis 基差

The spot price minus the futures price, varying by delivery location and month; it is part of hedging cost, not a guaranteed gain.
