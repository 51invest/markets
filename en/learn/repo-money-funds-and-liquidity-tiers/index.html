Repo, Money Funds and Liquidity Tiers

Short-term cash instruments all look cash-like, but collateral, redemption, valuation and counterparties differ; liquidity tiers emerge under stress.

Definition

Repo is short-term borrowing secured by securities: the borrower sells collateral and agrees to buy it back at a set price; the repo rate reflects funding cost and the haircut reflects collateral risk. A money-market fund holds short-term debt, repo and commercial paper and offers daily liquidity, but it is not a bank deposit and is not deposit-insured [2]. Commercial paper is unsecured short-term paper whose credit depends on the issuer's ability to roll it [3].

Intuition

Think of short-term cash instruments as four ways to get money: a Treasury bill is selling a certain IOU, repo is borrowing for a few days against that IOU as collateral, a money fund packages short debt and allows daily redemption, and commercial paper is an unsecured corporate IOU. In calm times all four feel like cash; in stress the differences appear: collateral is haircut, a fund may impose redemption fees, and paper may fail to roll. 'Liquidity tiers' simply mean that one yuan held in different instruments converts to usable cash at different speeds and prices.

Mechanism

Repo's safety buffer comes from the haircut: CNY 100 of collateral with a 2% haircut supports CNY 98 of cash; if collateral falls to CNY 95, the lender faces a CNY 3 gap and may call margin. Money-fund stress comes from asset-liability mismatch: assets become less liquid while investors can redeem daily; the fund may impose redemption fees, suspend redemptions or use a floating NAV. Commercial paper is unsecured, so a failed roll directly triggers a liquidity crisis. The usual short-end liquidity order is: Treasuries and overnight repo first, then regulated money funds, then unsecured commercial paper and repo from weaker issuers.

Worked example

Suppose CNY 100 of Treasuries is used in a one-day repo with a 2% haircut and a 2% annualised repo rate. The borrower receives CNY 98 and repurchases at 98 × (1 + 2%/365) ≈ CNY 98.005. If the collateral falls to CNY 95, the amount lendable at a 2% haircut is CNY 93.1, so the lender calls about CNY 4.9 of margin or additional collateral. If a money fund holds 20% commercial paper, a redemption wave may force sales at a discount and push NAV below CNY 1. These are teaching assumptions and exclude fees and tax.

Second worked example: money-fund NAV stress

Suppose a money fund has CNY 100 million of assets, including CNY 20 million of commercial paper, and NAV of CNY 1.00 per unit. In stress, 25% of investors redeem CNY 25 million, so the fund must sell assets. If it first sells CNY 5 million of paper at a 5% discount, the loss is CNY 250,000 and the remaining CNY 97.5 million implies NAV of about CNY 0.9975; at a 10% discount the loss is CNY 500,000 and NAV about CNY 0.995. If paper is 40% of assets and 30% is redeemed, forcing CNY 12 million of paper sales at a 10% discount, the loss is CNY 1.2 million and NAV about CNY 0.988. This is NAV arithmetic only; redemption fees, management fees and further declines in remaining assets are excluded. In real stress, redemptions reinforce one another.

Boundary and mistakes

First, 'redeemable daily' does not mean 'redeemable at par daily': market impact, redemption fees and suspension clauses change the outcome. Second, collateralised repo is not credit-risk-free: haircuts, rehypothecation and counterparty default can all cause losses. Third, a money fund is not a deposit and is not covered by deposit insurance. Fourth, higher commercial-paper yields usually reflect rollover risk, not a risk-free premium. Fifth, short duration does not prevent liquidity risk from amplifying within days.

Failure modes

The first failure is treating 'daily redeemable' as 'redeemable at par daily'. Fund documents may allow redemption fees, suspension or a floating NAV, so in stress investors do not get the stated one yuan. The second is looking only at collateral value in repo and ignoring haircuts and rehypothecation: if collateral falls 5% while the haircut rises from 2% to 10%, lendable value falls twice, and the gap is much larger than the price move. The third is assuming commercial paper always rolls: paper is short-term only while the issuer can refinance; if the market closes, maturity becomes a cash gap. The fourth is equating short duration with low risk; liquidity risk can amplify within days. The fifth is ignoring counterparty concentration: several funds may depend on the same dealers and the same collateral.

Three checks

First, ask what the instrument legally is: a deposit, a fund share, a secured loan or an unsecured IOU? Second, ask the cash-flow order under stress: which triggers first — collateral haircut, redemption fee, redemption suspension or a failed paper roll? Third, ask whether counterparties and collateral are concentrated: what share depends on one dealer, one paper issuer or one collateral pool? Write these three into a stress table; it is closer to real liquidity than 'short duration, high rating'.

Adjacent concepts and a counterexample

Repo is often described as a 'collateralised loan', but collateral management, rehypothecation and margin calls differ. A bank loan may accept receivables and inventory; repo usually accepts only highly liquid securities, marks them daily and applies a haircut, so margin calls arrive faster when collateral falls. A money fund is often described as 'like a deposit', but it is a fund share, NAV can float, redemptions can be limited and it is not deposit-insured. A counterexample is 'short duration equals low risk': rate risk is low, but liquidity and credit risk can amplify within days, especially when the fund concentrates in one type of commercial paper or one set of dealers. Compare short-end instruments on collateral, redemption rights, valuation frequency and counterparty concentration together.

Scope and limits

This repo and money-fund framework applies to short-end instruments with standard collateral, daily marking and clear central clearing or bilateral pledge. If collateral cannot be valued daily, the counterparty is concentrated, or the fund holds many illiquid assets, haircuts and redemption assumptions must be rebuilt. It does not apply to treating repo rates as risk-free, nor to inferring the future from historical NAV stability. Check collateral quality, haircut, rehypothecation and redemption gates, then recompute NAV under stress; normal-period data cannot be extrapolated to a redemption wave.

What the product is: rights and cash-flow structure

Repo is collateralised short-term funding: the cash lender buys collateral securities and agrees to sell them back at a set price, while the borrower exchanges securities for cash. The repo rate is the price of that funding. A money fund packages many short-term claims into redeemable shares.

During the repo, the cash lender holds collateral but usually does not take economic ownership; the borrower keeps the right to repurchase. If the borrower defaults, the lender can liquidate collateral. Money-fund holders can redeem at NAV, but fund terms may impose redemption caps, liquidity gates or suspension.

Participants, trading, holding and exit

Participants include dealers, money funds, bank and corporate treasuries, tri-party repo platforms, custodians and clearing firms. Dealers make markets, money funds supply cash, custodians hold collateral, and tri-party platforms match and settle.

Repo can be overnight, term or open; money funds operate through subscriptions, redemptions, NAV and a pool of short-term assets. On exit, repo must return collateral or run a default auction; money funds face redemption waves, asset discounts and liquidity gates.

Costs, risks and terms and documents

Costs: Repo rate, collateral haircut, tri-party fees, custody fees, settlement-failure cost and fund management fees. A one-point haircut can make effective funding cost far above the quoted repo rate.

Risks: Collateral price risk, counterparty risk, rollover failure, collateral rehypothecation, money-fund runs and redemption gates. Bigger maturity mismatch makes liquidity shocks easier to transmit.

Terms: Key documents are the repo master agreement, collateral list and haircut table, tri-party rules, fund prospectus and redemption terms. Check default handling, rehypothecation permission, valuation frequency and early-termination clauses.
