The Steel Supply Chain

From iron ore, coking coal and scrap to crude steel, hot-rolled coil and rebar, cash flows, costs and risks pass through each processing stage.

LevelFoundation

What the product is

“Ferrous metals” in this article means iron, manganese, chromium and their alloys, with steel as the most important product - a usage note written by this article, since none of the registered sources defines the term. Steel is not a single homogeneous commodity: grade, strength, surface treatment and dimensions determine its use and delivery standard. Investors can trade steel futures or hold mining and steel-company shares; physical businesses buy iron ore, coking coal, scrap and energy, then organize smelting and rolling.

Supply chain and participants

The blast furnace–basic oxygen furnace route relies mainly on iron ore and coking coal: ore is sintered or pelletized, coal is coked, the blast furnace reduces iron ore to hot metal, and the basic oxygen furnace converts it to steel. The electric arc furnace route relies mainly on scrap and electricity. Crude steel is continuously cast and rolled into hot-rolled coil, rebar and wire rod for construction, machinery, automobiles and appliances. Mines, coke plants, mills, traders, processors, warehouses and end users form the chain; freight, inventory and payment terms drive cash flow at each stage.

In 2025, world iron ore production was about 2.6 billion tons of usable ore containing about 1.6 billion tons of iron [1]. USGS also reports in its Events, Trends, and Issues narrative, rather than in the production table, that imported iron ore fines of 62% iron content on a CIF basis at Tianjin Port, China averaged $99.07 per ton in the first nine months of 2025, down from $112.07 in the same period of 2024 [1].

Trading, holding and exit

Steel futures can be closed or delivered. On the Shanghai Futures Exchange, the hot-rolled coil contract is 10 metric tons per lot, quoted in RMB per metric ton, with a minimum fluctuation of RMB 1 per metric ton, a minimum margin of 4% of contract value, and a 300-metric-ton delivery unit; the standard product is Q235B or SS400 coil 5.75 mm thick and 1,500 mm wide[3]. Rebar futures deliver grade HRB400E under GB 1499.2-2024, use the same 10-metric-ton lot size, carry a minimum trading margin of 5% of contract value and a 300-metric-ton delivery unit[4]. Operating businesses hedge to lock in processing margins, but basis and deliverable grade still create risk.

Costs and risks

Steel costs consist of iron ore, coking coal, scrap, power, transport and carbon. World crude steel production was about 1.849 billion tons in 2025, with 69.4% from the basic oxygen furnace route and 30.3% from electric arc furnaces; building and infrastructure accounted for about 52% of global steel use[2]. A property or infrastructure slowdown compresses demand, while trade barriers and capacity changes alter regional price spreads. Futures leverage, margin calls, delivery quality and inventory devaluation are real risks.

Terms and documents

Physical contracts cover grade, dimensions, weight, delivery location, payment and inspection. Futures contracts cover specifications, margin, last trading day, deliverable brands and warehouses. Equity holdings require attention to mineral rights, costs, output and capital spending. Iron ore from different origins differs in grade and impurities and cannot be compared by tonnage alone.

Comparison and boundaries

The blast furnace–basic oxygen furnace route depends on iron ore and coking coal, while the electric arc furnace route depends on scrap and electricity; their cost and carbon structures differ. Hot-rolled coil and rebar have different uses and delivery standards and are not interchangeable. Steel futures are not iron ore futures, and neither is a steel-company share: they represent a finished product, a raw material and corporate equity respectively.

Exercises

  1. Identify the route. Which steelmaking route uses scrap and electricity as its main inputs?
  2. Contract calculation. Assume hot-rolled coil costs RMB 3,500 per metric ton. Calculate the value of one 10-metric-ton contract and the 4% minimum margin.
  3. Demand conditions. Why does building and infrastructure investment have a large effect on steel demand?
Suggested answers
  1. The electric arc furnace (EAF) route.
  2. Contract value is 10 × 3,500 = RMB 35,000; minimum margin is 35,000 × 4% = RMB 1,400. This is a hypothetical price and excludes commissions and any broker margin add-on.
  3. Building and infrastructure accounted for about 52% of global steel use in 2025; changes in construction starts and investment directly affect demand for rebar, sections and flat products.
